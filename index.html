About

Who I am

I'm a Construction Engineering student at Iowa State University with an emphasis on the mechanical side of the field. My goal is to get good at optimizing infrastructure: figuring out where a process is wasting time or money, then rebuilding it so it doesn't.

That interest didn't start in a classroom. It started with a family iMac, a YouTube channel, and a stubborn habit of not accepting that something couldn't be done. I installed Windows on a Mac because the software I wanted wouldn't run otherwise. I taught myself Blender in a weekend because someone mentioned there was money in it. I bought a car with a blown head gasket because I wanted to know if I could fix it.

A few of those turned into real businesses: reselling repaired consoles and custom-built gaming PCs, then a Roblox development group that has pulled in millions of visits across its titles. Along the way I've run teams of developers and editors, which is where the project-management side of my brain got its first real workout.

These days I'm applying that same approach to construction: quantity takeoffs, scheduling, cost tracking, and finding better ways to move data between systems without losing half of it. The subject changed. The method didn't.

Hardware and resale

Console repair and gaming PC builds

This is the one that started everything else. Right after I got my job at Life Time, the PS5 and Xbox Series X launched, and both shipped with the same weak point: the HDMI port was poorly seated and would fail. So I learned to desolder and reflow the port back onto the motherboard.

That skill let me buy broken, nearly-new consoles for very little, repair them, and resell them at a real margin alongside my day job. Once I had capital, I moved up to building gaming PCs, sourcing discounted parts, keeping a stock of cables and cheap components, and assembling machines that cost less to build than they sold for.

After a few months of that, I had enough saved to justify buying a project car.

Swimming

Twelve years in the water

The thing that shaped how I handle everything else.

I started swimming at eleven, which is late by competitive standards. Before that it was soccer, mostly because my dad loved it, and neither my brother nor I ever really did. What my parents cared about was that we played something that would challenge us and build discipline, so they landed on competitive swimming.

I started at the Woodstock Dolphins, which ran lessons for beginners. Working up through the ranks into the competitive practices was a completely different animal. Anyone who's done it knows what I mean: swim practice teaches you to focus because it doesn't give you any other option.

That carried through every team I swam for after: SAGE YMCA, the YMCA in DeKalb, Reach Aquatics, and my high school team. The 6 AM practices are what shaped me most. Summer practices outside in the cold were brutal in their own way. Swimming changed me physically, but the part people miss is the mental side, doing the same set over and over, for hours, in conditions nobody would choose.

It paid off. At twelve I qualified for the state championships in the 200 fly, and more followed from there. I closed out my career by breaking my high school's 100 fly record and going to state with my relay team. After that I decided the era was over and hung up the cap.

I still swim occasionally with the club team at Iowa State, though most of my training now is calisthenics and just staying active. I don't regret a single part of it.
