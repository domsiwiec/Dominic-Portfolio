How it started

While I was streaming, someone in my audience floated an idea: make UGC items and sell them in the Roblox marketplace under the same niche my channel already covered. I had never touched 3D modeling before.

I gave myself a day. Over about 24 hours I taught myself Blender from scratch, modeled a handful of items, and published them under my group. Those items went on to earn roughly 6 million Robux.

Why that mattered: Roblox development has a real barrier to entry. Publishing a game idea outright runs about $1,700 through their exchange system, so having currency already built up is what lets you actually ship. The UGC revenue became the foundation for everything after it.

The Robux economy

The marketplace rewards people who already hold currency, which makes the first pile of Robux by far the hardest to earn. Once I had it, I could fund development directly instead of waiting on payouts, and that changed what I was able to attempt.

I'd already spent a long stretch learning Lua inside Roblox Studio, so I could build systems myself rather than paying someone to build every piece. Between the currency and the code, I could fund a title, outsource the parts that weren't a good use of my time, and keep moving.

Running a team

Once revenue justified it, I started outsourcing work: modelers, scripters, and editors, coordinated around what I actually wanted built. That's where the project-management side of this got real. Scoping a feature, handing it off with enough detail that it comes back usable, reviewing the result, and keeping several people moving at once is a completely different skill from building it yourself.

It's also the most directly transferable thing I've done. The vocabulary changes on a construction site, but scoping work, tracking it, and chasing down blockers is the same job.

Where it stands

Before AI tooling got good, I'd stepped back from hands-on development and focused on coordinating other people. Now that the tooling is genuinely useful, I've started building again myself, using it to move faster and cleaning up the details it gets wrong.

The revenue from this work is also what funded my 2019 WRX, which is a strange sentence to write, but that's how it happened.
